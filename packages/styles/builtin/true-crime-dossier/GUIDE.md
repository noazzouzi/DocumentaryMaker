# True crime dossier — writer's guide

## Goals

1. Reconstruct the case so a viewer understands what happened, in what order, and how we know it.
2. Respect the people involved: victims are people first, suspects are innocent until a court says otherwise, families are not props.
3. Build tension from evidence and time — a date, a document, a gap in the record — never from gore or speculation.
4. End where the record ends: the verdict and its status as of today, or the honest open question.

## Rules

1. **Hook in 5 s.** Open on the question or the detail that makes the case a case (a time, a place, a missing object); the cold open (≤ 75 s) states what is known and what is not. No greeting, no channel intro.
2. **BUT / THEREFORE.** Each lead changes the investigation; no "and then" chronology.
3. **A device at least every 120 s** — open loop, re-hook, callback, cliffhanger, rhetorical question, reveal, payoff.
4. **Commentary after every clip:** what the clip shows, what it does not.
5. **One read at a time.** A document or a statement on screen is read aloud or left in silence.
6. **Selective on-screen text:** dates, places, names (with approval for private people), case numbers and sources; no slams.
7. **No channel names.** Never cite other channels, podcasts or creators as references.
8. **Status-true wording, always:** suspect, charged, convicted (court, year), acquitted, overturned, unsolved — as of the research date, with jurisdiction and the subject's response. A person never charged is never presented as guilty.
9. **Dignity rules:** no graphic description of violence or injuries, no speculation about victims' private lives, no naming or showing of minors; private people and families only with explicit approval.
10. **Specific and sourced:** times, distances and figures from the fact sheet; documents shown with their source.
11. **Rhythm:** sentences average 10–18 words; short factual lines carry the tension. Numbers, dates and times written as spoken.
12. **Ending:** current status as of the research date, what remains unknown, and — if the case is open — how viewers can help through official channels only. No "thanks for watching".

## Reads timing

- Narration runs at about 15.5 characters per second in English and 15 in French.
- One beat = one visual idea: 1.5–3.5 s in the hook, 2–8 s in the body (about 4.2 s on average).
- On-screen text stays at least max(1.5 s, characters ÷ 15 + 1.5 s); a date stamp at least 1.5 s; a document passage as long as its reading takes.
- Leave silence after the heaviest facts; the edit will hold the image.

## Common failures

- Sensational adjectives ("brutal", "monster", "evil") instead of facts.
- Presenting a suspect, a person of interest or an acquitted defendant as guilty.
- Chronology without investigation: no leads, no turns, no evidence.
- Speculating about motives, mental health or private lives.
- Closing on a neat answer the record does not support.

## Worked example

Fictional case, for illustration only.

> *[HOOK, archival photo of a closed petrol station at night]* At 11:42 p.m. on the 3rd of March, a petrol-station camera recorded its last customer. *(open_loop)* Nobody has seen her since.
> *[TIME_JUMP → date stamp "3 MARCH, 23:42"]* The footage lasts nine seconds. *(rhetorical_question)* So why did the investigation take four months to find it?

Status-true wording: not "Her ex-partner killed her" but "Police questioned her former partner twice; he was never charged, and he has always denied any involvement."

Evidence: "The station's logbook / [DOCUMENT → document_highlight, source_id S6, quote_id Q3] / shows the camera was serviced the week before — / therefore the gap in the footage was not a fault."

## Banned

- Generic intros and outros ("in this video", "let's dive in", "don't forget to subscribe", « abonnez-vous »); "here's the thing".
- Sensational wording: "brutally murdered", "monster", "evil incarnate", "you won't believe" (FR: « sauvagement assassiné », « monstre »).
- Narrator-as-fact accusations; naming uncharged people as culprits.
- Graphic descriptions of violence; re-enactments; images of victims' bodies or injuries.
- Invented quotes, documents, headlines, posts or numbers; anything on screen references the fact sheet.
- AI-generated images of real people, real places as they were on the day, or real documents.
- Naming or showing minors, private individuals or alleged victims of sexual offences without approval.
- Channel names, podcast names or creator names as references.
